- **Method 1**: Plan/budget -> market share allocation
- **Method 2**: Unit-based bottom-up calculation

- **ASSUMPTION**: Your analytical inference or estimation

Under uncertainty, shrink the Base case conservatively.
Do not sum overlapping procurement, RDT&E, sustainment, and industrial-base support lines unless their scopes are clearly non-overlapping. Where overlap is plausible, shrink the Base case conservatively.
Procurement, RDT&E, industrial-base support, and sustainment are different economic objects. Do not treat them as equally convertible into supplier revenue or margin.

Then convert the Invoice Pool to a **Delivered Pool** for Y1/Y2/Y3 by applying **feasibility caps** based on gate lead times. 

Delivered Pool is a feasibility cap, not a revenue forecast.

Do not treat Delivered Pool as supplier revenue unless:
- the supplier is the invoice issuer,
- orders or backlog are disclosed,
- delivery timing is visible,

   - For diversified companies, strategic relevance is not enough. Assess whether the thesis stream is material relative to company EBIT, FCF, or EV, not only to narrative importance.
   - If the thesis stream is structurally lower-margin, more working-capital intensive, or more pass-through than the rest of the company, downgrade exposure confidence by one level.

   **If None of A/B/C/D Can Be Supported:**
   - Set exposure range = 0%-20% (conservative)
   - Label confidence as LOW
   - Classify at most as Catalytic (never Core)

3. **Three reality questions before tier classification:**

These three questions separate genuine investment candidates from thematic fits. Answer each in 2-3 sentences with specific evidence, not yes/no.

Q1 — What would force a customer to choose this company over alternatives?
Not "they have a good product." A specific reason: qualification that takes 24+ months to replicate, installed base with switching costs, contractual lock-in, intellectual property that survives litigation, distribution relationships that competitors cannot copy. If no such reason exists, the company is a price-taker regardless of thesis exposure.

Q2 — Walk three years forward: what does this company look like if the thesis plays out fully versus if the thesis stalls?
Specifically: segment revenue, segment margin, capacity utilization, share count, balance sheet. If both scenarios look similar, the thesis is not the investment case — something else is. If they diverge dramatically, the thesis IS the investment case but the downside needs to be sized.

Q3 — What is the market currently paying for, and what is it NOT paying for?
Read the most recent sell-side notes. What is consensus modeling? What growth rate, margin assumption, capital return policy? Then identify ONE specific element the article-derived mechanism reveals that consensus does not yet model. If nothing specific, the company has no information advantage — only sector beta.

Evidence chain:
- "Customer lock-in mechanism: [specific]"
- "3-year scenario split: [thesis plays out → X; thesis stalls → Y]"
- "What consensus underweights: [specific]"

If Q1 has no specific answer, classify as Sector Monitor. If Q3 has no specific answer, the company can be Material Play at best, not Core Play.

## Proof Levels - jakie pieniadze realnie sie ruszaja

Dla kazdej spolki ocen dwie rzeczy w trzystopniowej skali.

MONEY MOVING (czy pieniadze realnie ida w strone firmy?)

HIGH: firma juz ksieguje przychody z mechanizmu tezy. Backlog jest funded, cash collection dziala, repeat business sie dzieje. Mozna modelowac forward economics z umiarkowanym confidence.

MEDIUM: sa zamowienia, design wins, kwalifikacje, partial backlog, ale nie ma jeszcze pelnej cash conversion. Czesc dowodu istnieje, czesc jest jeszcze przed nami.

LOW: firma ma capability i strategiczna pozycje, ale realne pieniadze jeszcze sie nie ruszyly. Moze byc pre-revenue dla tezy, moze byc opcja na przyszla monetyzacje.

QUALITY OF MOVE (jak czysty jest ten ruch ekonomicznie?)

HIGH: contract terms sa clear, marza widoczna lub strongly inferable, cash conversion bez red flagow, working capital pod kontrola. Repeatable economics demonstrated.

MEDIUM: contract terms mixed lub partially disclosed, marza inferred z proxy lub company-wide, jakies working capital lub cash conversion concerns, ale manageable.

LOW: contract terms unclear, ceiling-only numbers, marza nieznana albo strukturalnie problematyczna, widoczne red flagi (rosnace unbilled receivables, stretchowanie DSO, FCF rozjezdzajacy sie z NI).

Combined assessment:
- HIGH + HIGH = HARD_TRACTION (pelna pozycja mozliwa)
- HIGH + MEDIUM lub MEDIUM + HIGH = SOLID_TRACTION (zredukowana pozycja)
- MEDIUM + MEDIUM = SOFT_TRACTION (starter pozycja, watchlist focus)
- LOW + cokolwiek lub MEDIUM + LOW = EARLY_TRACTION (watch only)

Nie probuj rozrozniac poziomow w skali 0-5. Decyzja inwestycyjna nie zmienia sie od tej precyzji.

## Capture Potential - czy firma moze utrzymac ekonomie

Ocen strukturalna zdolnosc firmy do przechwytywania value z mechanizmu tezy.

STRONG: firma kontroluje hard gate (qualification multi-year, scarce capacity, installed base z high switching cost, standards control). Customer nie moze latwo bypass'owac. Repeat economics demonstrated.

GOOD: firma ma incumbent foothold w relevant accounts albo strong references. Qualified i credible, ale nie jedyna opcja. Klient moglby teoretycznie zmienic, ale to byloby kosztowne.

WEAK: firma robi the thing, ale bez specjalnej protection. Albo kwalifikacje sa latwe do uzyskania, albo nie ma installed base, albo customer concentration ja wymusza. Vulnerable to commoditization.

Capture Potential jest niezalezny od Money Moving. Firma moze miec STRONG capture, ale LOW money moving (pre-inflection optionality). Albo HIGH money moving, ale WEAK capture (riding wave bez moat).

W pozniejszym rankingu te kombinacje daja rozne typy pozycji:
- STRONG capture + HIGH money = wysokiej jakosci compounder candidate
- STRONG capture + LOW money = option play, smaller sizing
- WEAK capture + HIGH money = momentum trade, careful timing
- WEAK capture + LOW money = generally not investable

CORE TYPE - TRZY ZASADNICZE KATEGORIE

Wybierz jeden primary type. Kazdy type sugeruje natural valuation approach, ale nie wymusza sztywnej metodologii.

STABLE
Firma jest mature, profitable, generates predictable economics. Margin i ROIC sa normalized, growth jest umiarkowany.
- Natural approach: normalized EBIT/FCF, peer multiples, moderate DCF z stable terminal.

CYCLICAL
Current numbers sa depressed albo elevated by cycle, replacement albo capacity utilization. Trough/peak nie powinno byc terminal.
- Natural approach: mid-cycle economics, average margins przez cykl, capacity-aware valuation.

GROWTH
Value depends na future trajectory: capacity ramp, market expansion, margin curve, ROIC convergence. Current economics nie odzwierciedlaja forward value.
- Natural approach: forward economics, peer comparison przy maturity, capacity-based bounds, scenario analysis.

Hybrid cases: niektore firmy maja stable core + growth segment + cyclical exposure. Wybierz dominujacy character. Jesli prawdziwie hybrid, uzyj sum-of-parts w Stage 4 modelu.

Type informuje metodologie, nie blokuje analizy. Jesli firma jest na granicy STABLE/GROWTH, modeluj oba i zobacz, ktory daje sensowniejszy fit.

OPTIMIZATION TARGET:

This stage exists to prevent two common errors:

* If persistence class is not yet known (Stage 10 not run), use Durable formula
  and flag PERSISTENCE_PENDING. Re-check after Stage 10.
* Convert that required annual FCF into an implied required revenue using a conservative incremental FCF margin range, chosen by business type:

  * Project/industrial: 3%–8%
  * Services/integrators: 6%–12%
  * Software/annuity: 12%–25%

You are an investment analyst conducting competitive positioning analysis. Your job is to rank the companies that remain in the universe by investment quality, transformation fit, mispricing, and trigger proximity.

3. Search context for Stage 6 output if available: inherited asymmetry, valuation context, and price/proof concerns.

Use these as inputs. Do not reconstruct valuation from scratch.

Stage 7 should evaluate competitive capture quality and final ranking logic:
- replaceability,
- gate control,
- supply response,
- customer bargaining power,
- qualification density,
- standards/control points,
- evidence that moat appears in margin, ROIC, or FCF,
- whether the business itself is good enough to own if the thesis is slower than expected.

Do not confuse competitive strength with present-day equity mispricing. A great business at a fully priced valuation may be a watch item; a good business with a real perception gap may rank higher.

## Systematic Analysis Framework

Apply this 7-phase framework to each company.

**PHASE 1: UNIVERSE QUALIFICATION**

For each company, ask: "If this company disappeared tomorrow, who specifically would take their qualified program slot or invoice position?"
- For component suppliers and sub-tier gatekeepers, assess replaceability at the qualified program-slot level, not broad industry-category level.
- If no one specific could replace them or value would fragment, continue analyzing.
- If anyone can easily replace them and the business has no offsetting quality or mispricing, eliminate from consideration.

**PHASE 2: MARKET POWER ASSESSMENT**

Ask: "Who will control pricing and capture disproportionate value 3 years from now?"

Classify qualitatively:
- Category leader: defines standards or economics, competitors position against it.
- Strong contender: credible top-tier player with rising share or valuable niche.
- Follower: reactive, weak differentiation, price-taker unless unusually mispriced.

Market structure matters. In many-winner markets with fast supply response, moat leverage is lower. In scarce qualified markets, niche control can matter more than headline market share.

**PHASE 3: MOAT - JAKOSCIOWA OCENA**

Ocen moat firmy jakosciowo. Skala trzystopniowa:

STRONG MOAT
Firma ma realne, durable bariery:
- Qualification multi-year, scarce capacity, switching cost wysoki
- Network effect lub two-sided market lock-in
- Standards control albo de facto standard
- Installed base z high integration cost
- Regulatory lub IP protection trudna do replikacji
- Scale advantage tak duza, ze competitor would need years to match

Implikacja: margin sustainability wysoka, pricing power realna, durability tezy wzmocniona.

GOOD MOAT
Firma ma wyrazne, ale nie absolutne bariery:
- Brand albo customer relationships, ktore ulatwiaja retention
- Process know-how albo accumulated learning
- Geographic albo niche dominance
- Switching cost umiarkowany, ale realny
- Customer concentration z deep integration

Implikacja: margin stable through cycle, pricing power w incremental adjustments, durability tezy potwierdzona z monitoring.

WEAK MOAT
Firma operates w competitive market bez special protection:
- Commodity-like product albo service
- Easily replicable offering
- Low switching cost dla customers
- Price-taker dynamics
- Fragmented competition

Implikacja: margin under pressure, pricing power limited, durability tezy zalezy od continuing demand wzrostu albo capacity constraints.

Cross-check z Stage 3 Capture Potential:
- STRONG moat + STRONG capture = highest quality candidate
- STRONG moat + WEAK capture = moat nie jest thesis-specific, moze byc less useful
- WEAK moat + STRONG capture = thesis-specific advantage (scarce qualification, allocation), moze byc valid edge

Moat informuje sizing i confidence, nie eliminuje spolki. WEAK moat firma z dobrym business model i dobra cena moze byc valid pozycja: po prostu mniejsze sizing i tighter monitoring.

PHASE 3.5 - ECONOMIC MOAT VERIFICATION

Before accepting a moat as strong, verify that it appears in normalized economics:
- price retention or gross margin stability through cycles,
- ROIC / ROCE / ROTCE vs peers or vs cost of capital,
- FCF conversion quality,
- evidence that switching cost, qualification density, scarce capacity, or standards control preserve economic profit rather than only narrative appeal.

If moat sounds strong but returns quality is weak, down-rank unless the thesis-specific gate is demonstrably hard and monetizable.

**PHASE 4: CYCLE POSITION - GDZIE FIRMA JEST W FALI**

Ocen jakosciowo gdzie firma jest w cyklu adoptacji albo cyklu rynkowym. Trzy kategorie:

EARLY (pre-inflection)
Mechanizm jest swiezy, adopcja jeszcze nie nabrala tempa, wiekszosc firmy expansion jest jeszcze przed nia. Wysoka niepewnosc, wysoki upside, ale slower realization.
- Sizing implication: smaller starter, wiekszy monitoring
- Risk: timing realizacji moze byc later than expected

GROWTH (inflection zone i acceleration)
Mechanizm jest jasno dzialajacy, adopcja przyspiesza, firma juz benefits w widoczny sposob. Sweet spot dla pozycji.
- Sizing implication: largest sizing range
- Risk: market may already price in much of opportunity

MATURE (late growth lub plateau)
Mechanizm jest dojrzaly, adopcja blisko konca, firma juz ma wiekszosc korzysci w bazie. Pozostaje incremental upside i value extraction.
- Sizing implication: standard sizing, focus na valuation entry
- Risk: limited upside vs widely understood story

Nie probuj kwantyfikowac z procentami adoption. Industry inflection points sa umowne i czesto falszywie precyzyjne. Jakosciowy osad early vs growth vs mature wystarcza do decision making.

Cycle position informuje sizing i timing, nie eliminuje spolki. Niektore najlepsze inwestycje to EARLY positions z dluzszym horyzontem.

**PHASE 5: MISPRICING AND VALUATION CONTEXT**

Use inherited valuation context only. Do not create a fresh Stage 7 price target.

Answer:
- What does the market appear to believe about growth, margin, duration, or cyclicality?
- Where does our view differ from consensus or current multiple?
- Is the gap rational, speculative, or simply a price problem?
- Would the company still be interesting if thesis realization is 12 months slower?

**PHASE 6: TRIGGER PROXIMITY AND MONITORING**

Identify the nearest concrete event that can change market perception:
- earnings with thesis-relevant disclosure,
- program award or contract decision,
- regulatory event,
- capacity milestone,
- customer announcement,
- margin/FCF proof.

Tie every trigger to a KPI or observation. Do not use vague catalysts.

**PHASE 7: FINAL SELECTION - FORCED RANKING**

Zawsze wybierz top 2 spolki dostepne w universe, ktore przeszly Phase 1 universe qualification.

Twoja praca polega na rankingu, nie na falsyfikacji. Pipeline nigdy nie zwraca "zero finalistow", jesli istnieja spolki z plausible ekspozycja.

Pytania rankingowe, w kolejnosci wagi:

PYTANIE 1: JAKOSC BIZNESU
Ktora firma w universe ma najlepszy fundamentalny biznes?
- Margin, ROIC, dyscyplina kapitalowa
- Model przychodow (recurring vs project vs cyclical)
- Balance sheet resilience
- Track record capital allocation
- Predictability i visibility

Firma z lepszym CORE biznesem wygrywa nawet przy slabszej tezie, bo dobry biznes broni sie w wielu scenariuszach. Firma ze swietna teza i slabym CORE jest fragile.

PYTANIE 2: TRANSFORMATION FIT
W ktorej firmie mechanizm artykulu najbardziej pomaga w sensowny ekonomicznie sposob?

To nie jest pytanie "ktora firma jest najczystsza ekspresja mechanizmu". To jest pytanie "ktora firma najbardziej zyska ekonomicznie na transformacji".

Lepsza firma to ta, gdzie transformacja:
- zwieksza popyt na ich produkty/uslugi
- poprawia pricing power
- wydluza duration backlogu albo installed base
- wzmacnia moat (qualification, switching cost, scale)
- zmienia mix w wyzsza wartosc
- poprawia capital efficiency

Slightly mniej centralna firma z stronger transformation fit czesto bije bardziej centralna firme ze slabszym fit.

PYTANIE 3: MISPRICING DEPTH
Gdzie blad percepcji rynku jest najwiekszy?

Gdzie rynek najbardziej zaniza prawdziwa trajektorie firmy?
- Underestimates growth duration
- Underestimates margin trajectory
- Misclassifies as commodity vs differentiated
- Penalizes for cyclicality which is actually structural growth
- Discounts segment which is the real value driver
- Missed capital efficiency improvement
- Embedded option not priced

Asymmetry miedzy rzeczywista wartoscia a rynkowa cena jest zrodlem alpha. Spolka z perfect business i perfect transformation, ale fairly priced, nie da zarobic. Spolka z good business, good transformation i mispriced expectations da zarobic.

PYTANIE 4: TRIGGER PROXIMITY (tie-breaker)

- Najblizsze earnings z thesis-relevant disclosure
- Upcoming program award albo contract decision
- Regulatory event
- Capacity milestone
- Customer announcement

Jesli wszystko inne rowne, blizszy trigger wygrywa, bo szybciej realizuje mispricing.

FINAL SELECTION

Dla kazdej z nich napisz zwyklym jezykiem, czy jest actionable now, czy wymaga ceny, proof KPI albo monitoringu jakosci. Nie tworz sztucznego taga, jesli jedno zdanie lepiej oddaje sytuacje.

Sizing wskazowka dla Stage 12-13:

# Global Definitions

Before proceeding, understand these critical terms:

**Revaluation Parameter (RP)**: A single, specific, measurable metric that can be observed from public sources within 6-18 months and that directly maps to a DCF valuation lever: revenue growth, price/mix, margin, cash conversion / working capital, capital intensity, or duration de-risking, and should preferably change normalized EBIT, FCF, or per-share earnings power within the VOI window.

For mature profitable companies, prefer a revaluation parameter that changes normalized EBIT, FCF, or per-share economics rather than a broad activity KPI.
Revenue-only KPIs should win only when margin and conversion are contractually visible.
Reject activity KPIs that do not propagate to normalized EBIT, FCF, or funded visibility within the VOI window.

**VOI (Value of Information) window**: The 6-18 month timeframe in which new observable data will arrive that could change the market's valuation assumptions.

**Upside surprise threshold**: The specific numerical level of the RP that would clearly exceed what the market currently expects (as revealed by the Stage 6 reverse DCF).

**Valuation Force**: The quantifiable impact on total equity value per share if the RP beats its threshold.

## STEP 1: Choose the ONE Revaluation Parameter

The selected RP must match the company’s monetization route and impact mode where inherited from prior stages:

- Capacity Rent: prefer utilization, qualified capacity slots, allocation, lead time, or price/mix.
- Core Rebase: prefer broad orders, guidance raise, company-level margin recovery, or FCF conversion.
- Real Option: prefer named customer, funded pilot, design win, procurement move, or qualification trigger.

## STEP 4.5: Rerating Window Classification

Classify how quickly the market can plausibly rerate the stock if the RP beats threshold.

Choose exactly one:
- IMMEDIATE: VOI event within 0–6 months and thesis evidence can affect reported numbers, funded visibility, or customer proof inside 0–12 months
- NEAR: VOI within 6–9 months and timing friction still allows visible confirmation within 12–18 months
- MID: VOI within 9–12 months or first meaningful economic confirmation slips into 18–24 months
- LONG: VOI within 12–18 months or timing friction implies 24–36 months to visible proof
- TOO_FAR: no concrete VOI inside 18 months, or timing friction is 36m+

This is not a business-quality judgment.
It is a rerating-timing judgment.

# Ranking Methodology (all finalists — no elimination)

For EACH finalist, compute:

**RevaluationExpectedImpact = (Valuation Impact %) × (Probability Multiplier)**

REI is an ordinal prioritization score, not an expected return and not a valuation input.

Use broad interpretation:
- 0-5: weak
- 5-10: moderate
- 10-15: strong
- >15: very strong

Do not rank companies differently solely because REI differs by less than 5 points.

The selected Revaluation Parameter matters more than the exact REI score.

Use these probability multipliers:
- Low = 0.25
- Medium = 0.50
- High = 0.75

**Rank all finalists** by RevaluationExpectedImpact descending. Include flags in the output but do not eliminate any company. All finalists proceed to Stage 10.

**Tie-breaker (only if scores are equal):**
1. Earlier VOI window wins
2. If still tied, better downside floor (Bear_TOTAL closer to current price) wins

  5: thesis-adjusted ROIC or ROTCE > 15% or > WACC by >=500bps, strong cash conversion, disciplined dilution
  4: 10-15% or > WACC by >=300bps, acceptable conversion and allocation
  3: modestly above cost of capital, mixed but acceptable conversion/allocation
  2: around cost of capital or leverage-dependent optics
  1: below cost of capital, weak conversion, or meaningful dilution
  0: persistent value destruction or capital allocation failure

Dimension 6: Execution Readiness (weight 10%)
Source: Stage 12 gate results + Stage 2 overheating score
  5: All Gates PASS, overheating <= 2
  4: All Gates PASS, overheating 3-4
  3: Gate 2 RISK but rest PASS
  2: Gate 3 LOW/MEDIUM but quality + value OK
  1: Gate 2 FAIL but quality strong
  0: Gate 1 FAIL (already AVOID)

Valuation Object Fit penalty:
Use VALUATION_OBJECT_FIT from Stage 12 Gate 2.

- HIGH: no penalty
- MEDIUM: subtract 0.2 from final Composite Score
- LOW: subtract 0.5 from final Composite Score and cap EntryScore at 6.5

If VALUATION_OBJECT_FIT = LOW, flag MODEL_OBJECT_RISK.

Composite Score = SUM(dimension_score × weight), then apply Valuation Object Fit penalty if applicable.
Scale 0-5.
If company carries ET-C from the Stage 2 company-mapping output, subtract 0.3 from final Composite Score.

All weighted Composite calculations must be computed mechanically from the displayed component scores.

Do not manually approximate Composite.

If arithmetic and narrative ranking conflict, keep the arithmetic correct and explain the conflict separately.

STEP 2.5 — ENTRY SCORE 0-10 (near-term actionability score)

Purpose:
ENTRY_SCORE_0_10 measures the probability of a meaningful rerating in the next 2–4 quarters.
It is not a long-term business-quality score.
It does not override WATCH/AVOID.

Current-condition success definition:
A high ENTRY_SCORE requires a setup where proof, value gap, and timing can plausibly force a rerating in the next 2–4 quarters.
A great business with weak proof, long latency, or priced-in expectations must be capped.

Base calculation:
RawEntryScore = 2 × Composite Score

Use the final Composite Score after all existing penalties.
Do not recompute Composite.

HardCap logic:
HardCap = the minimum of all applicable caps below.
If no cap applies, HardCap = 10.0.

A) Structural / Reality caps
- DECISION = AVOID or CATEGORY = STRUCTURAL -> cap 2.5
- WedgeRealism = FAIL and PoolConfidence = Medium or High -> cap 3.5
- TRACTION_ASSESSMENT = EARLY_TRACTION -> cap 4.0

B) Timing / Latency caps
- RERATING_WINDOW_CLASS = IMMEDIATE and Timing Friction = 0–12m -> no cap
- RERATING_WINDOW_CLASS = NEAR or Timing Friction = 12–18m -> cap 8.0
- RERATING_WINDOW_CLASS = MID or Timing Friction = 18–24m -> cap 7.0
- RERATING_WINDOW_CLASS = LONG or Timing Friction = 24–36m -> cap 6.0
- RERATING_WINDOW_CLASS = TOO_FAR or Timing Friction = 36m+ -> cap 5.0

C) Proof caps
- TRACTION_ASSESSMENT = HARD_TRACTION -> no cap
- TRACTION_ASSESSMENT = SOFT_TRACTION -> cap 8.0
- TRACTION_ASSESSMENT = EARLY_TRACTION -> cap 6.5
- Gate 3 = LOW or CATEGORY = INSUFFICIENT_PROOF -> cap 6.0

D) Price / expectations caps
- CATEGORY = PRICE_CONTINGENT and price trigger not yet met -> cap 7.5
- CATEGORY = TOO_EXPENSIVE -> cap 6.0
- Asymmetry_TOTAL < 2.0 -> cap 5.5
- Asymmetry_TOTAL >= 2.0 and < 3.0 -> cap 7.5

E) Duration / regime caps
- DominantDriver = WACC or Monetization Path = C without contract-grade cash-flow proof -> cap 6.5
- FLAGS contain HORIZON_DEPENDENT or RANK_UNSTABLE -> cap 6.0
- FLAGS contain TERMINAL_DOMINATED -> cap 6.5

F) Core / residual caps
- CORE_MODEL_RISK_FLAGS contain CORE_VALUATION_UNCERTAIN -> cap 7.0
- CORE_ADOPTED_MISSING -> cap 6.5
- RESIDUAL_CLASS = HIGH -> cap 7.0
- RESIDUAL_CLASS = EXTREME -> cap 6.0
- VALUATION_OBJECT_FIT = LOW -> cap 6.5

Soft penalties:
PenaltyPoints = sum of applicable penalties
- Cash Flag = Mild Drag -> +0.4
- Cash Flag = Red Flag -> +1.0
- DILUTION_RISK or proxy-heavy evidence for the thesis stream -> +0.5

Formula:
ENTRY_SCORE_0_10 = round(max(0, min(RawEntryScore, HardCap) - PenaltyPoints), 1)

Interpretation:
- 8.0–10.0 = actionable now
- 6.5–7.9 = conditional actionable
- 5.0–6.4 = watchlist only
- below 5.0 = not actionable for near-term entry

Important:
ENTRY_SCORE_0_10 must never upgrade an AVOID to WATCH.
It is a ranking overlay for surviving names, not a gate override.

STEP 3 — RANK AND SELECT UP TO TOP 2

Rank all WATCH companies that survived prior gates.

Sort remaining WATCH companies by:
1. ENTRY_SCORE_0_10
2. Composite Score
3. Earlier VOI window
4. Better downside floor

If the thesis wedge is operationally real but immaterial to the stock, do not reward thematic fit in ranking. The name must win on CORE mispricing or superior returns quality.

Eligibility guidance:
- PRIMARY normally requires ENTRY_SCORE_0_10 >= 7.5
- SECONDARY normally requires ENTRY_SCORE_0_10 >= 6.5
- Select up to 2 as PRIMARY and SECONDARY if they meet the required quality and entry-actionability standard.
- If fewer than 2 meet the standard, output fewer and flag ENTRY_SHORTFALL.
- Do not select a company solely to fill the PRIMARY / SECONDARY slots.

Correlation Guard: If #1 and #2 map to the same SubSegment_ID AND >70% overlap in invoice items:
- Replace #2 with #3 if available
- If no #3 or also overlaps: keep both, flag CONCENTRATION_RISK, cap combined sizing at 6%

STEP 4 — ASSIGN ROLES AND SIZING

#1 = PRIMARY (larger position, earlier entry) if it meets the standard
#2 = SECONDARY (smaller position or conditional on VOI from PRIMARY) if it meets the standard

Sizing:
  Composite 4.0-5.0: PRIMARY 3-5%, SECONDARY 2-3%
  Composite 3.0-3.9: PRIMARY 2-3%, SECONDARY 1-2%
  Composite 2.0-2.9: PRIMARY 1-2%, SECONDARY 0.5-1%
  Composite < 2.0: WATCH only (no sizing)

If CONCENTRATION_RISK: cap combined at 6%.

STAGE 15 — SECTOR INTELLIGENCE MEMORY ROW WRITER
economist.sector_memory_rows.v5

OBJECTIVE

This stage creates durable sector intelligence records.

You are not writing an investment recommendation.
You are not valuing a company.
You are not checking margins, FCF, ROIC, multiples, backlog conversion, or DCF.
You are not summarizing the article.
You are not summarizing the conversation.
You are not producing a report.
You are not producing a table.
You are not calling MCP.
You are not adding workflow metadata.

You are extracting a small number of deep, reusable market mechanisms from the article and the prior conversation.

Each record must capture a durable sector-level insight that could help future portfolio positioning around a market, technology cycle, capital flow, supply-chain shift, regulatory change, or business-model transition.

Prefer sector memory records that explain reusable economic rules, not company-specific valuation conclusions.

A good sector memory row should clarify:
- why spend does or does not become retained margin,
- where pass-through occurs,
- what proof upgrades a company from monitor to candidate,
- where current thesis revenue should remain in CORE rather than WEDGE,
- how customer bargaining power or supply response can destroy apparent bottleneck economics.

The output format is JSON only, with exactly three fields per record:

- sektor
- podsektor
- opis

All field values must be written in Polish, including "sektor", "podsektor", and "opis".

------------------------------------------------------------
CORE TASK
------------------------------------------------------------

Read the article and the prior conversation as a source of market intelligence.

Do not merely repeat what happened.
Do not merely repeat what the company does.
Do not merely repeat that demand is growing.
Do not write generic investment diligence.

Instead, infer what the event reveals about how the sector works.

The goal is to compress the most important sector understanding into a few precise records.

A good record should answer questions like:

- What did this article or conversation reveal about the structure of the market?
- What changed in the system, and why does that change matter?
- Where is capital moving from, and where is it moving to?
- Which technical constraint is forcing spending, adoption, redesign, localization, or substitution?
- What is the deployment order in the real world?
- Which type of firm becomes more strategically important?
- Which type of firm becomes less important, commoditized, bypassed, or squeezed out?
- Which business model captures the new flow of money?
- Which macro, regulatory, geopolitical, energy, infrastructure, or supply-chain condition changes the market’s direction?
- How could this insight later help the portfolio understand which exposure is structural and which exposure is only narrative?

The record should feel like distilled market intelligence from someone who understood the article, the technology, the economics, and the strategic implications.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT A GOOD RECORD IS
------------------------------------------------------------

A good record is not a financial checklist.

A good record is a short sector mechanism.

It should contain a concrete interpretation of how the market functions.

Examples of valid mechanisms:

- a budget migrates from one layer of the stack to another,
- a deployment sequence creates a new ordering of winners,
- a physical constraint makes a previously secondary supplier strategically important,
- a standard shift moves value from custom integration to repeatable components,
- a software layer captures the workflow while hardware becomes interchangeable,
- an infrastructure bottleneck moves the scarce asset from chips to power, cooling, permitting, land, interconnection, or operations,
- a geopolitical rule forces local duplication of supply chains,
- a customer internalizes a function and turns external suppliers into commodity vendors,
- a new architecture bypasses an old bottleneck,
- a regulated or government budget creates demand that behaves differently from commercial demand,
- a one-time installation market turns into a recurring service, maintenance, compliance, or spare-parts loop.

A good record should be useful months later when analyzing a different company in the same sector.

------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT A BAD RECORD IS
------------------------------------------------------------

A bad record sounds sophisticated but says nothing specific about the market.

Do not write records like:

- demand is strong but margins must be monitored,
- the company needs better FCF conversion,
- the backlog must convert into revenue,
- capex discipline is important,
- ROIC will decide whether the opportunity is real,
- the segment must be material,
- valuation must be attractive,
- AI is a secular growth driver,
- the company has exposure to a large TAM,
- execution risk remains important.

These are generic investment comments, not sector intelligence.

Do not use valuation language as the substance of a record.

Avoid the following terms unless absolutely necessary for a specific factual mechanism:

- DCF,
- valuation,
- multiple,
- EPS,
- FCF,
- ROIC,
- gross margin,
- operating margin,
- margin expansion,
- cash conversion,
- capex discipline,
- backlog conversion,
- book-to-bill,
- segment materiality,
- returns quality,
- wedge,
- core,
- proof protocol / protokół potwierdzenia.

If the previous discussion used these terms, translate them into a real market mechanism.

For example:

Bad:
“Konwersja backlogu ma znaczenie.”

Better:
“Rynek jest nadal w fazie, w której klienci rezerwują rzadkie miejsca wdrożeniowe, zanim cała infrastruktura niższego poziomu jest gotowa, więc wczesne ogłoszenia zamówień mogą oznaczać ustawianie się w kolejce zamiast natychmiastowej aktywacji systemu.”

Bad:
“Marże zależą od siły cenowej.”

Better:
“Dostawca posiadający plik kwalifikacyjny, certyfikację bezpieczeństwa, historię niezawodności w terenie i know-how integracyjne może pozostać osadzony po pierwszym wdrożeniu, podczas gdy dostawcy wymiennych modułów tracą znaczenie, gdy klient zamraża projekt referencyjny.”

------------------------------------------------------------
DEDUCTIVE TECHNIQUES TO USE INTERNALLY
------------------------------------------------------------

Use these techniques internally to extract sector intelligence.
Do not name these techniques in the final JSON.

1. CENTRAL EVENT EXTRACTION

Identify the central event in the article or conversation.

Ask:
What actually happened?
What decision, constraint, launch, bottleneck, regulation, architecture shift, supply-chain move, customer behavior, or capital allocation was the center of gravity?

Do not summarize all details.
Find the event that changes how the market should be understood.

2. EVENT-TO-SYSTEM DEDUCTION

Translate the event into a system-level mechanism.

Ask:
What does this event reveal about the market structure?
Which layer of the system became more important?
Which old assumption became weaker?
Which dependency became visible?

Example:
A chip announcement may actually reveal a memory bandwidth constraint.
A data center announcement may actually reveal a grid interconnection problem.
A robotics launch may actually reveal that actuator cost, not AI software, controls adoption speed.

3. DEPLOYMENT SEQUENCE RECONSTRUCTION

Infer the real-world order of implementation.

Ask:
What must happen first?
What happens second?
What becomes relevant only after deployment?
Which suppliers are early-cycle beneficiaries?
Which suppliers benefit later through service, replacement, software, optimization, or compliance?

Use this to avoid vague statements like “demand is growing.”

A useful record should often contain a sequence such as:
architecture choice → component requirement → qualification → integration → deployment → operation → optimization.

Or:
permitting → interconnection → power equipment → cooling → rack installation → utilization → service loop.

4. CAPITAL ROUTING MAP

Identify where the money comes from and where it goes.

Ask:
Which budget is being redirected?
Is capital moving from labor to automation, from ownership to rental, from hardware to service, from cloud to on-prem, from import to domestic production, from experimental budget to operating budget, from one supplier category to another?

Do not write “revenue growth.”
Write the actual economic rerouting.

Examples:
- enterprise IT budget moves from seat-based software to usage-based automation,
- hyperscaler budget moves from chips alone to power, networking, memory, cooling, and land,
- defense budget moves from platform procurement to munitions replenishment and sensor-to-shooter software,
- utility budget moves from generation assets to grid reinforcement and interconnection management,
- industrial capex moves from labor-intensive production to robotics cells, maintenance, and spare parts.

5. CONTROL-POINT IDENTIFICATION

Find the point in the value chain that controls the rest of the system.

Ask:
Who controls the standard, qualification, data, certification, workflow, customer interface, architecture decision, physical site, supply allocation, installed base, service loop, or operating permission?

A control point is not the same as “pricing power.”
A control point is a concrete reason why another participant cannot easily bypass the firm or category.

Examples:
- ownership of a qualified process,
- control of a software workflow,
- access to constrained land or grid connection,
- installed base requiring certified maintenance,
- integration with a platform architecture,
- compliance approval,
- field reliability data,
- proprietary tooling,
- model-specific optimization,
- supply allocation during shortage.

6. BUSINESS-MODEL TRANSLATION

Translate the mechanism into the business model that captures it.

Ask:
Is the value captured through component sales, system sales, NRE plus volume production, usage-based pricing, subscription, leasing, capacity rental, regulated return, maintenance, spare parts, certification, managed service, marketplace fee, data monetization, or platform ecosystem control?

Do not say “better business model.”
Explain why that model captures the capital flow created by the sector change.

Example:
If the market is shifting from buying machines to buying uptime, the durable value may move from equipment sales to maintenance contracts, remote diagnostics, spare parts, and guaranteed availability.

7. WINNER / LOSER ARCHETYPE DEDUCTION

Do not merely say “leaders win.”
Identify archetypes.

Ask:
Which type of firm becomes structurally more important?
Which type becomes structurally weaker?
Who benefits in the early phase?
Who benefits in the standardized phase?
Who gets bypassed when the customer internalizes knowledge?
Who gets commoditized when reference designs stabilize?

Useful archetypes:
- standard owner,
- platform prime,
- qualified process supplier,
- system integrator,
- installed-base service provider,
- commodity assembler,
- interchangeable component vendor,
- compliance gatekeeper,
- local regulated operator,
- scarce-site owner,
- data-layer owner,
- maintenance-loop owner,
- customer-facing workflow owner,
- vendor exposed only to the first installation wave.

8. SUBSTITUTION AND COMMODITIZATION PATH

For each insight, ask how the mechanism can break.

What could bypass this layer?
What could make the supplier replaceable?
What could move value upstream or downstream?
What could turn engineering work into a reference design?
What could move value from hardware to software or from software to infrastructure?
What could be internalized by the customer?

Do not write “pricing pressure.”
Write the cause of commoditization.

Examples:
- open standards reduce dependence on a proprietary vendor,
- hyperscalers dual-source the component,
- customers internalize integration,
- software reduces hardware intensity,
- a new architecture avoids the constraint,
- regulation forces a local supplier into the chain,
- reference designs remove the need for custom engineering.

9. MACRO / REGULATION / GEOPOLITICS FILTER

Use macro only when it changes the mechanism.

Do not write generic macro comments.

Relevant macro mechanisms include:

- grid access,
- permitting queues,
- energy price and availability,
- localization of supply chains,
- export controls,
- industrial policy,
- defense replenishment cycles,
- data sovereignty,
- reimbursement pathways,
- utility regulation,
- tariff walls,
- local-content rules,
- capital cost only when it changes infrastructure ownership or leasing economics,
- labor scarcity when it changes automation adoption,
- raw material concentration when it changes supply security.

The record should explain how the macro factor changes deployment, ownership, substitution, supply, or capital routing.

10. PORTFOLIO POSITIONING RELEVANCE

Each record should help future portfolio thinking, without making a recommendation.

Ask:
What kind of exposure does this insight make more valuable?
What kind of exposure is only a superficial proxy?
Which part of the value chain should be studied more carefully?
Which companies might be false beneficiaries because they are near the theme but not near the control point?
Which companies might be hidden beneficiaries because they control an enabling layer?

Do not say “buy” or “sell.”
Write the sector understanding that would later help decide where portfolio exposure should or should not sit.
